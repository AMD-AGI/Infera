### Important questions the report did not answer
1. **Does a running-share cap fight upstream's retraction-based design?** Because admission subtracts `retractable_tokens` (`decode.py:1271-1274, 485-494`), any ≥ 50 % rule must either change that term or gate admission separately. Otherwise the stock check will keep admitting against KV that the cap is supposed to protect. Where exactly does the gate go: `_required_admission_tokens`, the allocatable budget, or a count gate in `pop_preallocated`?
2. **How do the cap and deferred release interact?** Aborted-but-held KV (E1, active for Mooncake) is neither running nor in transfer. Does it count toward the "running" or the "in-flight" side of the 50 % split?
3. **Is the per-rank running cap of 16 the real C48 limit?** It was hit at C44 on one rank. Raising `max_running_requests` beyond 64 (and with it the per-rank graph ladder past 16) may matter more than KV share at C48. The report's "ladder up to 64" hid this.
4. **Is DP-rank imbalance the binding issue rather than the pool total?** Running counts differ by rank (max 16 vs 11 in X1). The share rule is per rank. Is it the busiest rank that needs protection?
5. **What does `prefill_complete` cost on our stack?** Write-back HiCache changes host-hit behaviour and removes chunk/transfer overlap, and no MLA/DSA numbers exist upstream. No estimate is given of the decode KV it would free at C44 (measurable from X1 `pending_prealloc_token_usage` and the transfer-queue token sums).
6. **What is the activation peak at 0.96?** The 10.89 GB headroom is post-capture but pre-load. The report does not cite the peak runtime usage observed at 0.95 under C40/C44 load, which decides whether 0.96 is safe.
