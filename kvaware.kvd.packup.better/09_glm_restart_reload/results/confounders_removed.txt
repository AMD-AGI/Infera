# Why this experiment's conclusion is confounder-free — the explicit argument.
#
# Numbers from results/step3_restart_reload.txt (2026-07-30 ~12:15-12:45).

===================== THE CLAIM =====================

  kvd delivered CROSS-RESTART KV reuse: a brand-new engine process read 170
  blocks out of a store that a previous, now-dead process had written.

===================== THE OBSERVATION =====================

Prefill node chi2879, kvd counters around the relaunch + replay:

                        BEFORE restart      AFTER restart+replay     delta
  entries                    340                   340               0
  host_bytes             600837120             600837120             0
  long_bytes             600837120             600837120             0
  sets_total                 340                   340               0
  gets_total                 170                   340            +170
  hits_total                 170                   340            +170
  misses_total                 0                     0               0

===================== THE THREE ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS, AND WHY EACH IS DEAD ===

Every one of these would produce "hits went up", so each has to be killed
separately. This is the part that makes the result worth more than the previous
prefix-reuse round, which killed none of them.

--- (a) "It was the GPU radix cache." ---

Dead by measurement. Before relaunching, VRAM was verified back at the idle
baseline:

  chi2879 GPU[0] VRAM used ... 297840640 B   (~297 MB)
  chi2867 GPU[0] VRAM used ... 297754624 B

An engine holding a 400 GB model with a populated KV pool does not sit at
297 MB. The process is gone and its VRAM — weights, KV pool, HiRadixCache — went
with it. The new engine started with an empty in-VRAM cache by construction.

This check is not a formality. It is the single precondition the whole
experiment rests on, which is why scripts/run.sh gates on it and refuses to let
a result be cited if VRAM is still high.

--- (b) "It was same-process reuse — it stored, then read its own writes." ---

Dead by process identity plus a flat sets_total. The writer process was killed
(`pkill -9 -f infera.engine.sglang`, confirmed `engine procs left: 0`); the
reader is a different PID that attached to the daemon afresh (8 new
"infera-kvd adapter connected" lines per leg).

--- (c) "The new engine just rebuilt everything and hit its own fresh writes." ---

This is the subtle one, and it is dead by **sets_total staying at exactly 340**.

If the new engine had re-computed and re-stored the prefix, sets_total would
have climbed by ~170 and entries/host_bytes with it. They did not move by a
single byte. The 170 gets were served entirely out of what was already there.

A rising gets_total on its own would NOT have been sufficient evidence. The
flat sets_total is what converts it into proof.

--- (d) "The kvd daemon restarted and reloaded from disk, so it's really an L3
        recovery test, not a live-store test." ---

Dead by pgrep: the daemon was never restarted.

  kvd alive on both nodes ..... pgrep -fc 'infera.kvd' = 1

(Note: kvd *can* recover its long region from disk across its own restart —
"long region recovered from /tmp/kvd-long" appears at daemon startup — but that
is a different capability and was not what happened here.)

===================== WHAT THE DECODE NODE DID, AND WHY IT DOESN'T MATTER ======

Decode node chi2867 was NOT clean:

                        BEFORE       AFTER      delta
  entries                  402         424       +22
  sets_total               402         438       +36
  gets_total               170         340      +170
  hits_total               170         340      +170
  misses_total               0           0         0

Its sets/entries moved. Not investigated. The most likely explanation is the
decode leg's own HiRadixCache repopulating decode-side blocks that were never in
kvd to begin with — but that is a hypothesis, not a finding, and it is recorded
as unresolved rather than smoothed over.

It does not affect the conclusion, because the conclusion rests entirely on the
prefill node's clean read-only delta. The prefill node alone exhibits all three
required properties simultaneously (gets up, hits up, sets flat), and one node
showing the clean signature is sufficient to establish that the mechanism works.

===================== WHAT THE RESULT IS NOT =====================

  Latency did NOT improve.  round 1 cold: 0.71 s median
                            round 2 reload: 0.76 s median

Reading ~6200 tokens of KV back over a unix socket from the host tier is not
obviously cheaper than recomputing prefill for a 6200-token prefix on 8x
MI355X. So kvd's demonstrated win here is CAPACITY AND SURVIVAL — the cache
outlives the engine — and explicitly not speed.

A latency win would need a much longer prefix, a slower prefill, or GPU-direct
reads. None of those were tested.
