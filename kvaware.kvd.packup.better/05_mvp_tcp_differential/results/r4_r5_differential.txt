=== SETUP THAT MAKES THE COMPARISON VALID ===

Both arms: both legs reached "ready to roll", the router saw both workers, KV
handoff completed, and NO completion returned HTTP 500. This matters — the
previous round's failure was HTTP 500 "Failed to get kvcache from prefill
instance" over real RDMA. MC_FORCE_TCP removed that, so both arms here are
genuinely answering, and the question moves from "does it transfer" to "is the
answer right".

An arm that never completed a request would say nothing about content. Neither
arm is in that state.


=== ARM A (r4) — kvaware ON, kvd ON ===

    Q "capital of France" -> 'v4 freddy\n\nAists. Log In andapace.a\n\nWho wouldin %%%%3...'
    Q "17*23"             -> 'S情辣梯neig治\n\n杖\n\n及格...'

Garbled. Not subtly wrong — word salad, mixed scripts, no relation to the
prompt.

The natural next move is to probe a PD leg directly to see which half is
producing nonsense. THAT DOES NOT WORK: curl to a PD leg's own port returns
nothing, because a PD leg only serves through the pair. A round was spent on
this before switching to the differential. The discriminating experiment is to
flip one thing and hold the rest.


=== ARM B (r5) — kvaware OFF, kvd OFF, round-robin ===

    Q "capital of France" -> 'v4ই\n\n脐猫\n\nument=""&gt;&lt; t-tcan you-...'

EQUALLY GARBLED.

And note: **both arms open with the same `v4` token.** Two runs with different
features, different router policy, different code paths through the KV plane —
and the same opening token. Whatever is generating this is upstream of anything
the switches control.


=== WHAT THE DIFFERENTIAL ESTABLISHES ===

The garbling belongs to the SAME-HOST PD + MC_FORCE_TCP substrate, not to
kvaware and not to kvd. With both features off the output is just as wrong.

That is a real result. It closed the question of whether the features were
breaking generation, and it did so for the cost of one extra ~2-minute round
rather than a deep dive into the KV plane.


=== WHAT THE DIFFERENTIAL DOES *NOT* ESTABLISH ===

  *** THIS IS A NO-REGRESSION OBSERVATION, NOT A CORRECTNESS PASS. ***

Spelled out, because this is exactly the sentence that gets over-quoted:

  * NOTHING here was correct. Arm A scored 0/4. Arm B scored 0/4.
  * "The features did not cause the garbling" is supported.
  * "kvaware+kvd produce correct output" is NOT supported. It is not even
    addressed. A test where both arms fail cannot certify either one.
  * It also does not establish that the features DID anything useful. Four
    short prefix-disjoint prompts give an offload path nothing to do; kvd's
    counters were not read in this round, and even if they had been, "connected"
    is not "serving".

The correct one-line citation is:

    "On same-host PD over MC_FORCE_TCP, output was garbled with kvaware+kvd ON
     and equally garbled with them OFF, so the garbling is not attributable to
     the features. Neither arm was correct; this is not a correctness result."


=== WHY THE INVESTIGATION MOVED TO TWO NODES ===

Two suspects remained after r5, and they were entangled:

  1. same-host PD (the pair co-located on one box), and
  2. MC_FORCE_TCP (only in use BECAUSE of #1 — same-host mooncake RDMA fails
     with "transport retry counter exceeded" / "received packet mismatch").

Because #2 exists only as a consequence of #1, no experiment on one node can
separate them. Moving to two nodes removes both at once: the pair is no longer
co-located, and real RDMA works, so MC_FORCE_TCP is no longer needed.

That was the right call for a second reason: a two-node run is the deployment
shape anyone actually cares about. The single-node MVP existed to shake out
wiring cheaply, and it did — three bugs and a fabric limitation in five rounds
of ~2 minutes each. Once the wiring was clean, the MVP had done its job.

UNKNOWN, and it stayed unknown: which of the two suspects is the actual cause
of the garbling. Both were eliminated in one step, so neither was ever
convicted individually. Nothing here diagnoses same-host PD or MC_FORCE_TCP
further; they were removed, not understood.
